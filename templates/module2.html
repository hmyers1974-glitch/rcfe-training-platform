Title 22 Navigation Lab

How to Use Title 22

One of the most important skills an RCFE administrator can develop is knowing how to locate a regulation instead of relying only on memory.

In this lab you will practice locating sections, searching by subject, identifying important regulatory words, and following cross-references.

Learning Goal

By the end of this section, you should be able to begin with a real facility problem and navigate to the part of Title 22 that may apply.

The RCFE Regulatory Road Map

Title 22 → Division 6 → Chapter 8 → Article → Section → Subsection

Think of this hierarchy as an address. “Title 22” gives you the general location, but you still need to narrow the search until you reach the actual section and subsection that applies.

Words That Change the Meaning

Finding the correct section is only part of the job. Read the exact regulatory language carefully.

Shall / Must
Signals a required action.
May
Can indicate permission or discretion.
Within
Look for a specific deadline.
Immediately
Timing is critical.
Except
Look for an exception to the general rule.
Unless / If
Look for a condition that changes how the rule applies.

Lesson 1 of 6

Title 22 Is a Decision-Making Tool

Administrators do not need to memorize every regulation. They need to know how to locate the correct authority and apply it to the facts.

Five-Step Administrator Method
  1. Identify the issue.
  2. Gather the facts.
  3. Locate the applicable authority.
  4. Apply the requirement.
  5. Document the decision and follow-up.

Lesson 4 of 6

Resident Rights

A family tells staff: “Do not let our mother leave with anyone unless we personally approve it.”

A family request does not automatically become a lawful facility restriction.

The administrator should consider:

  • The resident's wishes
  • Decision-making authority
  • Authorized representative status
  • Immediate safety concerns
  • Resident rights
  • Any lawful authority for the proposed restriction
Regulation Hunt

Find Personal Rights

Locate the personal-rights provisions before deciding whether a resident's movement or choices can be restricted.

Start in Article 8.
Search for Personal Rights.
Please type your answer before checking this hunt.
The administrator should evaluate the resident's wishes, authority of any representative, decision-making status, safety facts, applicable personal rights, and the legal basis for any proposed restriction.
Lesson 5 of 6

Defensible Documentation

Documentation should allow another qualified reviewer to understand what happened without needing the writer to explain it later.

Strong documentation includes:

  • Date and time
  • People involved
  • Direct observations
  • Statements and who made them
  • Actions taken
  • Notifications
  • Resident outcome
  • Follow-up
Regulation Hunt

Fact or Conclusion?

Which note is stronger?

A: “Resident was aggressive and out of control.”

B: “At 2:15 PM the resident threw a plastic cup toward the doorway, raised her voice, and stated, ‘Leave me alone.’”

Please type your answer before checking this hunt.
B is stronger because it documents observable behavior and the resident's actual words instead of replacing the facts with a broad conclusion.

Administrator Compliance Challenge
