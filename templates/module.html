CALIFORNIA RCFE LEADERSHIP ACADEMY

Module 1: Resident Down

Emergency response, resident assessment, and appropriate action after a fall.

Estimated learning time: 60 minutes

Learning Objectives

By the end of this module, you should be able to:

  • Recognize immediate priorities when a resident is found on the floor.
  • Avoid actions that could worsen a possible injury.
  • Direct staff through an organized assessment and emergency response.
  • Identify documentation and notification responsibilities after a fall.
  • Apply the response process to a realistic RCFE incident.

Episode 1

Resident Down

8:05 AM

You are the administrator on duty during the morning shift. You hear a caregiver calling for help from a resident's room.

🏥 Assisted Living Incident

Resident found on the floor. Administrator responds to the scene.

Caregiver: “I need help in Room 12. Mrs. Wilson is on the floor.”

You arrive at the room. The resident is lying on the floor beside the bed. A caregiver is standing nearby and says:

“I think she fell. Should I help her back into bed?”

Administrator Decision

What should you direct the caregiver to do first?

Leadership principle: A resident found on the floor should not automatically be lifted. The first priority is safety and assessment.
Episode 2

Secure the Scene

8:07 AM
👩‍⚕️ Administrator Response

Stay calm, control the scene, and direct staff.

You ask the caregiver to remain with the resident. Another staff member is asked to bring the resident's emergency information and vital-sign equipment.

You observe the room. A walker is several feet away. A bedside table has been moved. There is no obvious blood.

What should the administrator do?

Leadership principle: The administrator controls the scene, assigns staff roles, and prevents additional injury.
Episode 3

Resident Assessment

8:09 AM

Mrs. Wilson is awake but appears frightened. She tells you she is having pain in her left hip.

Resident: “Please don't move me. My hip hurts.”

You observe her breathing and responsiveness. You ask simple questions to determine whether she is alert and able to communicate.

Which response is most appropriate?

Remember: Pain, altered mental status, inability to move normally, bleeding, head injury, or other concerning findings may require emergency medical evaluation.
Episode 4

Emergency Response

8:12 AM

Mrs. Wilson continues to report severe hip pain. You determine that emergency medical evaluation is appropriate.

Administrator: “Please call 911. Stay with Mrs. Wilson and continue monitoring her. Bring her emergency information and medication information for EMS.”

Staff assignments

  • One caregiver remains with the resident.
  • One staff member calls 911.
  • One staff member gathers emergency information.
  • The administrator coordinates the response and prepares for EMS.

Why is assigning specific roles important?

Leadership principle: During an emergency, assign specific responsibilities instead of telling everyone generally to “help.”
Episode 5

EMS Arrival

8:18 AM

EMS arrives at the community. You provide the resident's information and explain what staff observed.

“Mrs. Wilson was found on the floor at approximately 8:05 AM. She reported left hip pain and has not been moved since staff discovered her.”

What information should accompany the resident?

Documentation principle: Provide accurate, objective information about what staff observed, what the resident reported, and what actions were taken.
Episode 6

After the Resident Leaves

8:35 AM

EMS transports Mrs. Wilson for evaluation. The immediate emergency is under control.

Now the administrator must transition from response to follow-up.

Follow-up may include: incident documentation, required notifications, physician/family communication as applicable, care-plan review, staff follow-up, environmental review, and prevention strategies.
Episode 7

Leadership Review

9:15 AM

You meet with the team after the immediate emergency response. The goal is not simply to determine that a fall occurred. The goal is to identify how future risk can be reduced.

Administrator: “Let's review what happened, what we know, what we don't know, and what we can change to reduce the chance of another incident.”

Final leadership question

Leadership takeaway: Every incident is an opportunity to strengthen resident safety, staff response, communication, and systems.
