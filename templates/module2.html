Module 2: Read It. Interpret It. Apply It.

Using Title 22 as an RCFE Administrator

Great administrators do not simply memorize regulations. They know how to find the right rule, read it carefully, connect it to the facts, and document a defensible decision.

6 Lessons • Regulation Hunts • Knowledge Check
Before You Begin

Learn the Road Map

When someone says, “It’s in Title 22,” that is not the end of the search. It is the beginning.

For RCFE regulations, train yourself to follow the regulatory hierarchy:

Title 22 → Division 6 → Chapter 8 → Article → Section → Subsection
Administrator Skill:

Do not stop when you find a section title that sounds right. Read the actual subsection. Look for words such as shall, must, may, immediately, within, except, unless, and if.

Lesson 1 of 6

Title 22 Is a Decision-Making Tool

Administrators are responsible for more than knowing that regulations exist. They must know how to use those regulations when supervising the facility.

The Five-Step Administrator Method
  1. Identify the issue.
  2. Gather the facts.
  3. Locate the applicable authority.
  4. Apply the requirement to the facts.
  5. Document the decision and follow-up.
Regulation Hunt 1

What Does Title 22 Expect From the Administrator?

Find the regulation that establishes the administrator's responsibility to operate the facility in accordance with regulations.
Start with the RCFE regulations. Try to locate the answer before opening the hints.
Start in Article 7 — Personnel.
Look for the section titled Administrator — Qualifications and Duties.
Look closely at the administrator's listed responsibilities, not only the qualification requirements.
Target: §87405 — Administrator — Qualifications and Duties.
Good. The key learning point is that the administrator has an affirmative responsibility to administer the facility according to applicable regulations and established facility policy. The administrator cannot simply delegate compliance and stop paying attention.
Lesson 2 of 6

Facts — Not Conclusions

A complaint is important information, but a complaint is not automatically a verified fact.

Scenario:

A family member tells you that their mother's call button was ignored “for almost an hour.”

A defensible investigation separates:

  • What was reported
  • What was observed
  • What records show
  • What witnesses remember
  • What remains unknown
Regulation Hunt 2

Where Would You Look for the Resident's Records?

Find the part of the RCFE regulations dealing with resident records.

Your goal is not to memorize every item in the record. Your goal is to learn where to go when you need evidence.

Move beyond Article 8 and look at the next Article.
Look in Article 9 — Resident Records.
Search for the section titled Resident Records.
Target: §87506 — Resident Records.
Exactly. An administrator should preserve the allegation as reported and then compare it with available evidence. The point is to build the conclusion from facts rather than writing the conclusion first.
Lesson 3 of 6

Read the Regulation Carefully

Small words matter in regulatory language.

  • Shall / Must — signals a requirement.
  • May — can signal permission or discretion.
  • Immediately — timing matters.
  • Within — look for a deadline.
  • Except / Unless — look for conditions.
Regulation Hunt 3A

Find a Reporting Deadline

Locate the RCFE reporting-requirements section. Find at least one event that requires a written report within a stated period of time.
Start in Article 4 — Operating Requirements.
Look for a section with the word Reporting in its title.
Pay special attention to timing words such as within, immediately, and next working day.
Target: §87211 — Reporting Requirements.
This is why administrators must read beyond the heading. Different reportable events can have different notification and written-report timing requirements.
Regulation Hunt 3B

Do Not Rely on “Licensing Told Me Years Ago”

An employee says: “Licensing told us years ago that this was okay.”
What should the administrator verify before continuing the practice?
Correct approach: verify the current regulation and any applicable current law, official guidance, waiver, exception, or facility-specific approval. An old verbal recollection should not replace current authority.
Lesson 4 of 6

Resident Rights

Scenario:

A family tells the facility:

“Do not let our mother leave with anyone unless we personally approve it.”

A well-intentioned family request does not automatically become facility policy.

Regulation Hunt 4A

Find Personal Rights

Where in the RCFE regulations would you begin before restricting a resident's freedom or choices?
Start in Article 8.
Article 8 addresses resident assessments, fundamental services, and rights.
Search within Article 8 for Personal Rights.
Target: the RCFE Personal Rights requirements, including §87468 and related personal-rights provisions.
Good. Determine the resident's wishes, decision-making status, authority of any representative, safety facts, applicable personal rights, and whether any restriction actually has lawful authority.
Regulation Hunt 4B

Dementia Does Not Mean “No Rights”

A resident with dementia repeatedly attempts to leave. Where else might you need to look in Title 22?
Go beyond the general personal-rights rule.
Look for the section on Care of Persons with Dementia.
Target: §87705 — Care of Persons with Dementia. Read it together with the personal-rights requirements.
Exactly. One regulation may describe dementia-specific safety measures while another protects resident rights. Administrators often have to read multiple sections together.
Lesson 5 of 6

Defensible Documentation

Good documentation tells the story without requiring the writer to explain it later.

Strong documentation usually identifies:

  • Date and time
  • Who was involved
  • What was directly observed
  • What was reported and by whom
  • Actions taken
  • Notifications made
  • Resident response or outcome
  • Required follow-up
Regulation Hunt 5A

Find the Resident-Record Rule

Locate the resident-record section and identify why complete records matter during a Licensing review.
Start in Article 9 — Resident Records.
Search for Resident Records.
Target: §87506 — Resident Records.
The record should allow a reviewer to reconstruct what occurred, what the facility knew, what it did, and what follow-up occurred.
Regulation Hunt 5B

Fact or Conclusion?

Which statement is stronger documentation?

A: “Resident was aggressive and out of control.”

B: “At 2:15 PM resident threw a plastic cup toward the doorway, raised her voice, and stated, ‘Leave me alone.’”

B is stronger because it records observable behavior and the resident's statement rather than replacing the facts with a conclusion.
Lesson 6 of 6

Administrator Compliance Challenge

Scenario:

A resident states that a caregiver refused to help her.

The caregiver says assistance was offered but the resident declined.

Another employee heard an argument but did not see how the interaction began.

Regulation Hunt 6A

Build the Regulatory Map

This scenario may require more than one regulation. Which areas of Title 22 would you consider?
Consider resident rights.
Consider resident records and documentation.
If the facts show an incident that threatens resident welfare, also examine reporting requirements.
Excellent regulatory thinking. A real event can involve multiple requirements at the same time. The administrator should avoid searching for one isolated rule and assuming the analysis is finished.
Final Regulation Hunt

Does This Need to Be Reported?

Do not guess. Go back to the reporting requirements and determine whether the verified facts fit a reportable category.
Go back to Article 4.
Find Reporting Requirements.
Target: §87211 — Reporting Requirements. Compare the verified event with the categories and timing requirements in the regulation.
Correct. First establish what actually happened. Then compare the verified facts with the regulatory reporting criteria and timing requirements. Do not decide based only on the label someone placed on the incident.
The Administrator's Compliance Formula:

FACTS → AUTHORITY → APPLICATION → ACTION → DOCUMENTATION → FOLLOW-UP
