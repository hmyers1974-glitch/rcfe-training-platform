Title 22: Read It. Interpret It. Apply It.

Regulatory interpretation, administrator judgment, resident rights, documentation, and defensible compliance decisions.

Estimated learning time: 60 minutes

← Back to Training Modules

Learning Objectives

  • Recognize when an RCFE issue requires regulatory review.
  • Separate assumptions from actual regulatory requirements.
  • Locate and interpret the applicable Title 22 requirement.
  • Apply regulations to real operational situations.
  • Protect resident rights while managing safety and risk.
  • Document administrative decisions in a defensible manner.
Administrator mindset: Your job is not simply to memorize regulations. Your job is to recognize the issue, verify the facts, identify the authority, apply it correctly, and document the decision.

LESSON 1

Title 22 Is a Decision-Making Tool

A Regulatory Question Lands on Your Desk

A caregiver approaches the administrator with a family request that may conflict with a resident's rights.

Checking video…

Administrators sometimes treat Title 22 as a giant list of rules that must be memorized. That often leads to poor decisions.

A better approach is to recognize when an issue exists and follow a reliable process.

The Five-Step Regulatory Process

  1. Identify the issue.
  2. Gather the facts.
  3. Verify the authority.
  4. Apply the requirement.
  5. Document the decision and follow-up.
Example:

A resident's daughter tells staff that her mother may not leave the building with a longtime friend.

The administrator should not automatically follow the request. The administrator must determine resident rights, decision-making authority, safety concerns, and whether a lawful restriction exists.

LESSON 2

Facts Before Conclusions

What Do We Actually Know?

Separate documented facts from assumptions.

Checking video…

Administrators should avoid reaching conclusions before determining what actually occurred.

Scenario:

A family member says a resident waited 30 minutes after pressing the call system.

The caregiver says the response was immediate.

Review objective evidence such as call-system records, care notes, staffing assignments, witness statements, and other documentation.

Evidence first. Separate what someone believes happened from what can actually be verified.

Words Matter

Shall, must, may, unless, except, immediately, and within can change the meaning of a requirement.

LESSON 5

Defensible Documentation

Document What Happened

Strong documentation is factual, timely, objective, and connected to follow-up.

Checking video…

Documentation should identify:

  • What happened.
  • When it happened.
  • Who was involved.
  • Who was notified.
  • What action was taken.
  • What follow-up remains.
Never rewrite documentation merely to make an incident “sound better.”
LESSON 6

Complaints and Conflicting Statements

When Stories Conflict

Preserve the facts first. Apply the requirement second.

Checking video…

The family demands the caregiver be fired immediately.

Facts first. Authority second. Application third. Documentation throughout.
