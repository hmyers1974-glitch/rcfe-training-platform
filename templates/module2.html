Learning Objectives

By the end of this module, you should be able to:

  • Recognize when an RCFE issue requires regulatory review.
  • Separate assumptions from actual regulatory requirements.
  • Locate and interpret the applicable Title 22 requirement.
  • Apply regulations to real operational situations.
  • Protect resident rights while managing safety and risk.
  • Document administrative decisions in a defensible manner.
  • Explain your reasoning clearly during a licensing review.
Administrator mindset: Knowing regulations is important. Knowing how to apply them correctly when the situation is unclear is what separates strong administrators from administrators who simply memorize rules.

LESSON 1

Title 22 Is a Decision-Making Tool

OPENING SCENARIO

A Regulatory Question Lands on Your Desk

Video 1 will appear here when uploaded.

Loading video...

Administrators sometimes treat Title 22 as a giant list of rules that must be memorized. That approach can lead to poor decisions.

Your job is not to memorize every regulation. Your job is to recognize when a situation raises a regulatory issue and know how to find, interpret, apply, and document the applicable requirement.

A strong administrator asks: What is the actual issue, what are the facts, what authority applies, and what action best protects the resident?

The Five-Step Regulatory Process

  1. Identify the issue.
  2. Gather the facts.
  3. Verify the authority.
  4. Apply the requirement to the facts.
  5. Document the decision and follow-up.
LESSON 2

Start With Facts — Not Conclusions

INVESTIGATION SCENE

Three Staff Members. Three Different Stories.

Separate observations from conclusions.

Loading video...

Licensing problems often grow because someone reaches a conclusion before determining what actually happened.

Conclusion:
“The caregiver neglected the resident.”
Facts:
“The resident activated the call system at approximately 2:10 PM. Staff entered the room at approximately 2:18 PM. The resident reported needing assistance to the restroom.”

Before deciding what regulation applies, ask:

  • What exactly happened?
  • When did it happen?
  • Who was present?
  • What did the resident need?
  • What did the care plan require?
  • What did staff actually do?
  • What documentation exists?
  • Was anyone notified?
  • Was the resident harmed or placed at risk?
LESSON 3

Read the Regulation Carefully

VERIFICATION SCENE

“Licensing Told Me Years Ago…”

Verify the current requirement.

Loading video...

A common compliance mistake is relying on what someone remembers Title 22 says rather than reading the actual requirement.

Pay attention to words such as:

  • shall
  • must
  • may
  • unless
  • except
  • immediately
  • within a specified period

LESSON 5

Documentation Makes Your Decision Defensible

DOCUMENTATION SCENE

Could You Defend This Decision Six Weeks Later?

Documentation should show what happened and why the decision was made.

Loading video...

Even a reasonable decision becomes difficult to defend if nobody can determine why it was made.

A defensible administrative note may include:

  • The issue identified.
  • Relevant facts gathered.
  • People interviewed or consulted.
  • Records reviewed.
  • Applicable regulatory requirement.
  • Resident-specific considerations.
  • Action taken.
  • Notifications completed.
  • Follow-up or monitoring plan.
Avoid documentation designed to make an event “sound better.” Documentation should be factual, objective, accurate, timely, and complete.
